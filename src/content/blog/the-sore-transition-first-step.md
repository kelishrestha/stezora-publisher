Change does not announce itself. It arrives as a door already closing behind you, and the only question left is which way your feet turn.

We like to believe we step into change, but we seldom do. We are usually mid-air when we notice the ground has moved. Somewhere between the person who packed the suitcase and the person who opens it in a stranger's kitchen, a small death took place — quiet, unobserved, and utterly final.

## The ache of becoming

There is a soreness that accompanies transition, a tenderness that lingers long after the event that caused it. It is the muscle memory of a life that no longer exists, reaching for handles that have been removed.

This essay is the first in a series about that ache — the part of becoming that no one photographs. Not the arrival, not the announcement, but the ordinary, unglamorous hours in which a person is unmade and slowly re-woven.

## Letting go is a skill

We speak of letting go as though it were an act of will, a single decisive motion. In practice it is a discipline. It is deciding, again and again, on the side of the future. It is choosing the empty room, the unfamiliar street, the version of yourself you have not yet met.

> Every transition asks the same question: what are you willing to leave unfinished?

The answer changes. That is the point. The first step is always leaving — and leaving, as it turns out, is not a place but a direction.
