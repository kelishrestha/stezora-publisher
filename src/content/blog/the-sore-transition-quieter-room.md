When the things you built to keep you safe become the things you have to dismantle, the quiet is not peace. It is a construction site after hours.

I spent a year removing my own scaffolding. Chairs that held conversations I no longer wanted. Books chosen to impress people I no longer saw. Routines that once felt like shelter and now felt like a script I was tired of performing.

## The unmaking

There is no ceremony for this kind of work. No one claps when you donate the furniture of your former life. The room simply gets quieter, and in that quiet you can finally hear the question you have been avoiding: *What is actually mine?*

What remained after the year was not much, and it was everything. A table. Two cups. A shelf of books I have actually finished. The people who do not require the script.

## What the quiet teaches

The transition was not the removal. The transition was the year afterward, learning to live with the echo, learning that empty and hollow are not the same word. One is a space. The other is a story you tell about the space.

The room is quieter now. Not silent — there is a difference. It is the difference between a house that is waiting and a house that has already chosen.
